Blindsight · cortical visual prosthesis

Learning what to stimulate,
so a camera becomes sight.

A camera image has to become safe currents on about a thousand electrodes in visual cortex, tuned to one person's brain. I built a differentiable simulator of the phosphenes stimulation evokes, then trained an encoder through it under hard current and channel limits, with a small per-patient adapter on top.

Read the paper (PDF) Try the live demo Results
There is no public human V1 stimulation dataset, so everything here runs in simulation. The simulator is checked against published human data (cortical magnification, brightness, thresholds), and the 1024-site "threads" layout is my own idealisation, not a Neuralink specification.

How it works

1 · A differentiable phosphene simulator

Electrodes sit on a flattened V1 surface and map into the visual field through the wedge-dipole model of cortical magnification. Current spreads through tissue to set each phosphene's size. Tissue activation (with habituation) goes through a sigmoid to set brightness, following van der Grinten et al. (2024), re-implemented from the equations with a closed form for a single pulse train. Each simulated patient has their own thresholds, phosphene-position jitter and dead electrodes.

2 · A position-aware encoder with hard safety limits

A small U-Net produces a retinotopic map that is read out bilinearly at each electrode's phosphene location, so it is not a dense 1024-way output. A safety layer clips current at 100 µA and allows at most 256 electrodes (25%) to be active at once. Training goes end to end through the simulator with a percept-reconstruction loss plus a charge penalty.

3 · A per-patient adapter

Everything that differs between people lives in a per-electrode gain and bias (2N parameters). It is fitted without labels to a patient's calibrated forward model, using 16 to 1024 images. The low dimension also makes it a good fit for human-in-the-loop preference tuning.

Limits

  • Simulation only. Real phosphenes vary in shape and colour, and change in ways this model doesn't capture.
  • CPU scale: the percept classifiers train on 6k images for 4 epochs, and ablations use one seed. run_gpu.sh runs the full scale.
  • At 50% unannounced electrode failure on natural images, the learned encoder falls slightly below intensity sampling (SSIM 0.235 vs 0.251).
  • Without the percept loss, a single Utah array, or an unconstrained run started at 100 µA, training collapsed to no stimulation. The paper reports these as findings.
