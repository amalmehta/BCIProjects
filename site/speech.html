Speech · attempted speech to text

From motor-cortex spikes
to words, on a laptop CPU.

A person who can no longer speak attempts to say a sentence. 512 neural features recorded from speech motor cortex every 20 ms go to a phoneme decoder trained with CTC, then a language model turns the phonemes into words. This is the full pipeline on public clinical-trial data, with calibration, robustness and latency analyses, trained under a CPU budget.

Read the paper (PDF) Watch a decode Results
Real data: the Brain-to-Text '25 release (Card et al., NEJM 2024, participant T15), 10 training sessions, 426 held-out sentences. Models were trained for only 480 steps on a shared CPU, so error rates are far above the published system's. The point is a complete, honest pipeline. run_gpu.sh runs the full 45-session scale.

Calibrating on a new day

This is a held-out session 77 days after the last training day, with word error on 48 sentences. Re-fitting only that day's input layer beats reusing yesterday's, and fine-tuning the whole network is no better.

How it works

Neural → phonemes

Each session gets its own affine input layer to absorb day-to-day drift. The features are then patched into 120 ms frames and fed to either the published GRU recipe (baseline) or a causal Conformer with self-conditioned CTC, where intermediate CTC predictions are fed back into later layers. Training uses white-noise and offset augmentation, time masking and electrode dropout. The output is 40 phonemes (CMUdict ARPAbet), a word boundary and the CTC blank.

Phonemes → words

A lexicon-constrained CTC beam search runs over a 44,485-word vocabulary with a Kneser–Ney trigram built from public text. Every corpus sentence that matched a dev or test sentence (68 of them) was removed to avoid leakage. An optional GPT-2 rescoring pass re-ranks the n-best list.

Latency

The neural decoder is cheap: 0.3 ms per 120 ms frame for the cached causal Conformer and 2.6 ms for the GRU, on 2 CPU threads. The language model is the bottleneck, at 210 ms per sentence for the beam search and 960 ms for GPT-2 rescoring. That is where a product would need to spend its engineering effort.

Limits

  • Models are trained for only 480 steps, so absolute error rates are far from the state of the art (the published system has a few % WER).
  • The Conformer result rests on one seed. One GRU seed stalled, and the 2-seed ensembles are worse than the good seed alone.
  • Several ablations were cut or trimmed because the shared CPU was saturated. All of them are listed in the paper and in gpu.yaml.
  • Dryad blocks scripted downloads, so the data came from a public Hugging Face mirror of the Brain-to-Text '25 release.
