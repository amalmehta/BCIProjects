Assisted Robotic Arm · reach and grasp

A decoder that's often wrong,
and an arm that helps anyway.

7-DoF decoding from motor cortex is noisy: the real multi-day Wiener filter here reaches R² 0.32. I decoded real human recordings (FALCON H1), then built a simulated 7-DoF Kinova arm whose shared autonomy infers which object you want. It accounts for how the decoder's errors behave, assists only when confident, and gives control back the moment you change your mind.

Results

Shared autonomy (simulated, 60 paired episodes per cell)

In paired McNemar tests, DASA beats the fixed, confidence and hindsight methods on clutter (p ≤ 0.0002) and beats distance blending (p = 4.7e-10) and hindsight assistance (p = 3.8e-6) on change of mind. Distance blending ties DASA on the standard task.

Offline 7-DoF decoding on FALCON H1 (real data)

Within session: FALCON's variance-weighted R² on the public minival split.

Across days: mean R² on 7 later days (6–20 days after training), by recalibration budget. This is our own split, because FALCON's held-out split is private.

Two findings worth stating plainly. First, each public held-in calibration file starts with exactly its minival bins. Left in, that leak inflates GRU R² to 0.80. They are stripped here, and a unit test enforces it. Second, fine-tuning a whole network on seconds of new-day data is worse than not adapting at all. Re-fitting only the read-in layer is the one recalibration that never hurts.

How it works

Decoding

Spikes from 176 channels go to Wiener, Kalman, GRU, causal Transformer and multi-day GRU decoders. The multi-day GRU has a per-day read-in layer and augmentation, so a new day needs only the read-in re-fitted, from as little as 10–45 s of data.

Decoder-aware shared autonomy (DASA)

Goal inference is Bayesian over the objects on the table. Unlike standard policy blending, the likelihood uses a calibrated model of the decoder's own errors, including their autocorrelation: consecutive decoded velocities are not independent evidence, and treating them as independent makes the posterior overconfident. A switching prior lets belief move when the user changes their mind, and a confidence gate sets the autonomy weight α. A safety filter enforces joint limits, workspace bounds, velocity caps and table clearance. Across 7,968 episodes it never had to block a table collision.

Limits

  • Simulated user and simulated arm (kinematic Kinova Gen3, DH parameters from the user guide), with no contact physics.
  • Small CPU models with 1–2 seeds. run_gpu.sh runs the full scale.
  • Cross-day numbers use our own split, so they aren't comparable with the FALCON leaderboard.
  • No peer-reviewed results from Neuralink's robotic-arm study exist yet to compare against.
