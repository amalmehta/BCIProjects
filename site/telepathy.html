Telepathy · cursor control

A cursor decoder that still
works four months later.

People with paralysis need a cursor that works when they sit down, without a daily calibration block, and what they feel is throughput, not offline R². I trained decoders on 5 days of a public 96-electrode motor-cortex dataset, tested them on 19 later days without labels, and built a closed-loop WebGrid simulator to score each one in bits per second.

Offline R² is on real recordings (O'Doherty et al. 2017, monkey Indy, 24 sessions over 138 days). Every bits-per-second number comes from a simulated user in closed loop, not from a person or an animal.

WebGrid, in closed loop (simulated)

WebGrid is the task Neuralink uses to report cursor throughput: a target cell lights up and the user moves to it and selects it. Bitrate = log₂(N−1) × max(correct − incorrect, 0) / time. Here a feedback-control "user" aims at each target, a neural encoding model fit to the recordings turns that intention into spikes, and each decoder turns the spikes back into cursor movement. Each replay is one 60-second session.

How it works

Stable Latent Decoder (SLD)

Each day gets an identity-initialised channel adapter, which feeds a shared recurrent latent model trained on five calibration days. Training uses input perturbations (gain, offset and electrode dropout) and a masked-spike reconstruction loss. On a new day the adapter is fitted without labels, by NoMAD-style likelihood plus latent-moment matching, or with a few labelled reaches.

What actually mattered

The ablations are blunt. Training on several days, perturbing inputs and re-scaling each channel with the new day's statistics do most of the work: a GRU with augmentation matches SLD on the regular days. Unsupervised alignment helps only far from calibration (82–138 days) and on the day-28 electrode re-mapping, where it recovered in 2 of 3 seeds. That is the honest result, and the paper reports it that way.

Closed-loop simulator

The simulated user is an optimal-feedback-control-style controller with motor noise. An encoding model fit to each day's recordings turns its intended velocity into spike counts. Gain, smoothing and dwell are tuned per decoder, as a clinician would. A ReFIT-style intention re-estimation roughly doubles the Wiener filter's simulated bitrate (1.27 → 2.43 bits/s).

Limits

  • Monkey data with 96 electrodes and a simulated closed loop. No human or animal was in the loop.
  • Simulated bitrates are noisy (4 × 60 s sessions per decoder), so differences under about 0.5 bits/s aren't meaningful.
  • The Kalman baseline is weak at 20 ms bins (R² ≈ 0.2). It was tuned but not re-engineered.
  • The machine was heavily loaded, so the CPU runs use fewer seeds and days for few-shot and ablations. run_gpu.sh runs the full scale.
